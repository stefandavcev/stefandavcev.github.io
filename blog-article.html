What a business website needs before design starts.

A strong website does not begin with colour choices or animations. It begins with decisions about audience, content, priorities and the action each page should support.

A template can make a website look complete long before the important thinking is complete. That is exactly how placeholder sections, duplicated pages and generic claims survive into live websites. The solution is not more decoration. It is a clearer content plan.

1. Define the job of the website

“We need a modern website” is not a useful goal on its own. A transport company may need quote requests and proof of reliability. A music group may need event discovery, media and booking contact. A restaurant may need atmosphere, essential information and an easy route to visit or connect. The design should reflect that job.

2. Prepare content by priority, not by page length

Before building layouts, identify the information visitors need first, what supports trust and what can remain secondary. Better content is often shorter because repeated statements and vague marketing language have been removed.

A useful starting document includes:

  • The main audience and what they are trying to find
  • The primary action: contact, quote, booking, visit or purchase
  • A realistic page list
  • Existing text, images and brand materials
  • Required languages, forms and integrations
  • Who will update the content after launch

3. Design the mobile path early

Many visitors arrive from social media, search or shared links on a phone. Navigation, headings, forms and calls to action should be planned for that context from the beginning rather than repaired after the desktop design is approved.

4. Decide what WordPress should make editable

Not every visual detail needs to become a complicated page-builder control. The useful question is which content the owner will genuinely update: services, projects, events, galleries, team members or contact information. A simpler editing experience is usually more sustainable.

5. Plan ownership and handover

A launch is not complete when the public page opens. Domain ownership, hosting access, WordPress users, form delivery, backups and update responsibilities should be clear. The client should not be dependent on hidden credentials or undocumented decisions.

A website becomes easier to design when everyone agrees what it needs to do.

Final checklist

Before the visual design begins, confirm the goal, audience, pages, content owner, required functions and preferred launch date. That small amount of preparation saves far more time than it takes.
